On November 18, 2025, I was laid off from my role as a Senior Frontend Engineer. As a "bonus," I had signed an IT mortgage just the day before. It's a subsidized mortgage program for tech workers in Russia, and to keep the reduced rate I had to find a job within 6 months at a company that met the program's requirements.

News that landing a job in IT was getting harder didn't worry me. For an experienced engineer with years in the field, the market hasn't changed, or so I thought. Reality proved me wrong pretty quickly.

I picked the 10 most relevant openings and sent my CV. Based on past experience, I figured that would be enough for 3–4 first-round interviews, which could turn into one or even two offers.

Three days passed. No feedback. Nothing at all. Most of my applications hadn't even been viewed.

I started sending 5 applications a day, then 10, then I stopped counting and just applied to everything that fit.

That's when I got the full taste of modern IT hiring:

More than half of applications get no response. Unfortunately, you have to get used to it.
Rejections with no explanation. I was often turned down even when my skills matched the requirements almost perfectly. Maybe my resume put recruiters off, or maybe some of the openings were never real.
Duplicate job posts. Identical openings, one for a senior and one for a middle engineer. Not a big deal, but it usually means you won't hear back.
The first conversation is almost always with a chatbot. I'm not against new technology, but writing detailed answers to a bot takes longer than a five-minute phone call.

Once, I answered an HR's questions and was then given a take-home assignment. When I asked what the next interview stages would be, I got a rejection.

During this time I realized the IT market has changed for everyone. To avoid getting frustrated for nothing, you need to be ready for questionnaires, conversations with AI and the fact that quick results aren't guaranteed. You can still find a job, but it takes more time and effort.
