In my previous post, I described the "joys" of job hunting in today's market. Here's how the story ended.

At first, I was looking for senior roles with the salary I wanted, at companies that qualified for Russia's subsidized IT mortgage program. Those openings ran out pretty quickly, and I started bargaining with myself: "What if I switch from the subsidized rate to the market rate?" "The pay is lower here, but the company qualifies." "What if I join as a middle engineer, with room to grow?" By then I had gone through almost every active opening, so I simply checked the job sites and applied to anything new. During this period, I didn't get a single interview.

More than three months into the search, I was feeling down. Not so much because of how long it was taking, but because there were no results at all and the outlook wasn't great.

My financial cushion had worn thin — more of a pillowcase than a cushion by then. I had to decide what to do next. I have teaching experience, so maybe private lessons, or going back to the university where I started my career? Becoming a freelancer and working for myself? Or signing up as a food delivery courier?

Freelancers and tutors need to build a client base and a portfolio, so at first you work almost for free. Nobody was waiting for me back at the university. Only the courier job promised a flexible schedule, steady income and plenty of fresh air.

And then I remembered my colleagues, former and current. That's where I should have started, but I had never used my network to find a job, so the idea didn't come to me right away.

I sent personal messages to all my colleagues. Many of them replied, and that felt good.

A couple of days later, I had an offer. The product I'll be working on has real users. It's a mature, self-sustaining application with tasks ranging from new features to refactoring legacy code, so there will be no time to get bored.

The company qualifies for the IT mortgage, and the requirements and tech stack are clear to me. Mission accomplished, even though it took far more time and nerves than I expected at the start.
